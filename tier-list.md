# Tier list

A first pass at ranking everything you can buy. Generated by `harness/tierboard.mjs` and graded
by hand where the harness has nothing to measure.

```bash
node harness/tierboard.mjs --weeks 4,9,13 --seeds 10 --spots 32 --out tiers.json
```

**Re-run and re-grade this whenever the catalogue, the cards or the ordinances change.** The
letters are quantiles of the ranking, not absolute thresholds, so they only mean anything against
a current measurement.

---

## How it was measured

Five fixed benches, so a tile measured today and a tile measured next month are measured against
the same crowd:

| Bench | Board | What is already standing |
|---|---|---|
| **amenity** | Terminal 12×12 | A Parking Lot at each end of the board and a Restroom either side of the middle. The crowd is all `$`. |
| **premium** | Terminal 12×12 | The same, with the far Parking Lot swapped for an Express Train, so about half the crowd is `$$$`. |
| **transport** | Terminal 12×12 | One Parking Lot and a four-shop chain (Newsstand, Food Stand, Coffee Shop, Burger Joint) down the middle. |
| **water / air** | Waterfront, Sky Harbour | The transport bench, rebuilt on those levels, because their own stock is sold nowhere else. |

The tile under test is swept over **every legal spot** in the free band between the transports
(the whole board, for a transport), and the headline number is the **median** spot's score delta
per $100 — not the best, because the player cannot see the best cell in advance. Every shop is
tried on both crowds and keeps the better reading: a Designer Shop among budget travellers is not
a bad tile, it is a tile in the wrong station, and the `bench` column says which station suited it.

Cards and ordinances have no footprint, so they are run as simulator modifiers on the same
benches. Cards that buy action points are priced at the median shop those points would buy, which
is the only honest exchange rate between a card that buys time and one that changes a rule.

Everything is averaged over weeks 4, 9 and 13, 10 seeds each, skipping the weeks a tile is not yet
on sale. `median` and `best` are in stars (1,000 points); `cash` is the week's extra revenue.

## Ordinances

Free and permanent, so there is no price to divide by. The number is the share of that week's
quota the ordinance adds, on the bench it suited best.

| Ordinance | Grade | Share of quota | Note |
|---|---|---|---|
| Staff Expansion | **S** | *not measured* | +1 action point every week for the rest of the run, at +15% prices. Overtime grades S for buying two points once; this buys one every week. Graded by hand. |
| Night Service | **S** | +46.6% | Six more ticks and four more spawn ticks against a 15% quota rise. The longest lever in the game. |
| Wayfinding Signs | **A** | +24.8% | +1 radius on every shop, −15% capacity. On a dense board the radius wins easily. |
| Tourist Board | **A** | +15.2% | Two-tier gaps pull at 0.6 instead of 0.25. Worth nothing on a single-tier board and a lot on a mixed one — it measured 0% on the budget bench and +15% on the premium one. |
| Express Charter | **A** | +13.3% | +0.25 on every exit multiplier, −0.10 on every shop's. Good on a transport-heavy board. |
| Zoning Variance | **B** | *not measured* | Half your money back on a demolition, and the action point is free. It changes what a mistake costs rather than what a week scores. Graded by hand. |
| Loyalty Scheme | **C** | +0.0% | One more stop, half fares. The stop budget is almost never what binds — the week is (§6.3) — so this is the half-fare clause with nothing attached. |
| Union Contract | **C** | −0.4% | +2 dwell, −20% capacity. Near enough free, and it does nothing for you. |
| Retail Compact | **D** | −9.1% | Double revenue, half the flat bonus. You are selling score for cash at a poor rate. |

## What the list says

- **Cheap tier-1 transport leads the field.** Parking Lot, Bike Rental, Bus Stop and the four
  level-exclusive water and air tiles all sit at 19–60★ per $100, against 13–21 for good shops
  and 4–7 for the big late transports. That is the intended shape: a platform is what feeds the
  chain, and the chain is where the points are, so a cheap feeder beats an expensive one.
- **Coupon Book is out of line** at 160★ per $100, five times the best tile and nearly three
  times the next card. Twice the crowd for two weeks at half fares barely costs anything, because
  fares are a small share of the score (`economy.fareScale` is 0.35). It is the first thing to
  look at if cards ever get a pass.
- **Action points are the real currency.** Overtime and Temp Staff grade S on nothing but the
  tiles they let you buy, and Staff Expansion is the best ordinance for the same reason.
- **Utility tiles grade low and that is correct.** WiFi Hotspot, Moving Walkway, Waiting Area and
  the Security Checkpoint are bought for what they do to the tiles around them; a bench with four
  tiles on it has almost nothing for them to do. Read them on `sensitivity.mjs` against a real
  board instead, where the Checkpoint's best spot is worth 48–59★.
- **Two D grades are placement, not power.** Frequent Flier Club reads 0.7★ at its median spot and
  11.8★ at its best, and Chrono Lounge 3.8 against 13.3. Both are lounges: they are worth nothing
  where nobody waits and a great deal on a platform's doorstep. A median is the wrong summary for
  a tile with that shape, which is why `best` is in the table.
- **Fast Pass measures at exactly zero**, and that is a genuine reading rather than a bench
  artifact: two more stops does nothing while the *week* is what binds, not the stop budget
  (§6.3). It would only matter on a board dense enough that a `$` traveller runs out of budget
  with time to spare.
- **The one-square shops landed where they were aimed.** Pocket Park 19.2 against Green Space
  21.0, Coffee Cart 19.1 against Coffee Shop 19.9: clearly less per tile, slightly less per
  dollar, far better per cell. On a 12×12 board the big version is still the better buy; on
  Junction's 9×9 or a Sky Harbour half, floor is the currency that is short.
- **Cash Machine is deliberately outside the ranking.** 10.9★ per $100 puts it in B, but $39 a
  week on one square is the best revenue per cell in the catalogue. It is bought for the till.

## Known limits of the method

- **The bench is four or five tiles.** Anything whose value comes from a long chain or a dense
  board is under-read here. Cross-check with `tierlist.mjs`, which uses the bot's own boards.
- **Only two crowds.** `$$` and `$$$$` travellers are only ever a minority of either bench, so
  tier-2 and tier-4 shops read slightly low.
- **Median hides bimodal tiles.** See the lounges above.
- **Nothing here prices an action point against a dollar** except by the median-shop convention
  in the harness. A card that saves a week is worth more late than early, and the list does not
  say so.
